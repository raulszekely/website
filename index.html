I am a research psychologist working at the intersection of the social and behavioural sciences and human-computer interaction. My research examines how digital tools and interventions, spanning web platforms, mobile apps, serious games, VR, and, increasingly, conversational agents, can be designed and evaluated to support health, learning, and wellbeing in ways that are accessible, equitable, and inclusive. I am particularly interested in how digital media and immersive technologies shape understanding of mental health and attitudes towards mental illness, how people engage with and place trust in AI-based conversational agents, and how the quality and safety of digital mental health tools can be assessed and assured. Across these areas, I seek to advance the science and practice of digital (mental) health by translating rigorous evidence into tools that make a meaningful difference in people's lives.

Methodologically, I adopt a participatory, user-centred stance, combining evidence synthesis, qualitative and mixed-methods inquiry, experimental designs, and co-design with users and stakeholders. I collaborate closely with people with lived experience, clinicians, educators, and developers through iterative cycles of design and testing, co-creating solutions that respond to users' needs and contexts. I focus especially on populations that digital health has historically underserved, including disabled and neurodivergent people, ethnic minority communities, and those affected by socioeconomic disadvantage or digital exclusion. I am committed to ensuring that digital innovations reduce rather than widen health inequalities.
