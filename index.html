About

I am a psychologist and researcher interested in how people experience technology and how technology affects behaviour, attitudes and health.

My research includes digital health, virtual reality and artificial intelligence. I am particularly interested in engagement, trust, stigma, representation and the ways technology can be made more accessible and inclusive.

My work draws on established psychological and behavioural theory, chosen to fit the question at hand — including the COM-B model (Capability, Opportunity, Motivation – Behaviour) and Behaviour Change Wheel, UTAUT (the Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology), the Person-Based Approach, and user-centred design.

I completed my PhD in Applied Psychology at the University of Surrey, where I examined the use of virtual reality to reduce mental health stigma among healthcare students. I am now a Senior Research Fellow in the School of Psychology at the University of Roehampton.

Research

My research examines how people interact with technology and how psychological research can improve the way technology is designed and evaluated.

  • Digital health

    Research into digital interventions for health and wellbeing, including how people use them, what affects engagement and how they can be designed around users' needs.

  • Virtual reality & immersive technology

    Research into how immersive technologies affect perception, experience, attitudes and behaviour, including their use in mental health.

  • AI, trust & behaviour

    Research into how people understand and respond to artificial intelligence, including trust, credibility, acceptance, safety and its use in mental health.

  • Stigma, media & representation

    Research into how mental health and health conditions are represented in media and technology, and how these representations affect attitudes and stigma.

  • Engagement & inclusion

    Research into why people use, avoid or disengage from technology, and how behavioural science, user research and co-design can help make technologies more accessible and useful — including for neurodivergent and disabled people.

Selected research

Horizon Europe · University of Roehampton

Together for Mental Health

A European project developing an evidence-based mental health toolbox for use in education and workplaces across seven countries. My work at Roehampton focuses on the UK case study and the development and evaluation of resources for secondary schools.

Innovate UK · Maudsley Learning

MTAQ — Mental Health Technology Assessment of Quality

A quality assurance framework for digital mental health tools, assessing areas including user experience and co-design, data privacy and security, and evidence and ongoing evaluation. The framework was developed through research with service users, healthcare and research professionals, and digital health experts.

Read the MTAQ paper
NIHR-SPCR · Queen Mary University of London

Diabetes Balance

Co-design of a web-based intervention to support emotional wellbeing in people with diabetes, including the development of a user engagement strategy and an implementation plan.

PhD research · University of Surrey

Virtual reality and mental health stigma

Research examining whether virtual reality could be used to reduce mental health stigma among healthcare students, combining research on stigma and attitudes with immersive technology.

Consultancy

I work with organisations developing, evaluating or researching digital technologies in health and healthcare. Whether you are shaping a research idea, developing a technology, planning an evaluation or assessing existing evidence, I can provide research expertise at different stages of a project.

Research advice & strategy

For teams that know what they want to find out, but want an experienced researcher to help shape the approach.

  • Framing research questions and study design
  • Choosing methods for qualitative, quantitative or mixed-methods research
  • Developing research protocols and evaluation plans
  • Interpreting findings and their implications
  • Providing research input throughout a project

Research & evaluation

For teams that want the research carried out directly, from a single defined activity to a full evaluation.

  • Co-design with users and stakeholders
  • User testing, think-aloud studies and interviews
  • Effectiveness research, including feasibility studies and pilot trials
  • Using behavioural science to diagnose engagement problems and design targeted improvements
  • Qualitative, quantitative and mixed-methods analysis
  • Research reports and recommendations

Evidence & quality

For teams that need to understand whether a technology's evidence, quality or research base stands up to scrutiny — including for regulatory approval, commissioning or market-access purposes.

  • Literature reviews, including systematic and scoping reviews
  • Evidence synthesis and mapping
  • Quality assessment of digital health tools
  • Identifying gaps in the existing evidence
  • Evidence-informed recommendations

Ethics & responsible practice

Good research also means being clear about what people are being asked to do, protecting participant wellbeing, handling data responsibly and being honest about what the evidence does and does not show.
